* Pseudo-instructions expand into real words

MIPS assembly lets you write convenient pseudo-instructions like =la= (load 32-bit address) and =ble= (branch if less than or equal), even though a 32-bit instruction word can only carry a 16-bit immediate. The assembler quietly rewrites them using the reserved assembler temporary =$at= before execution starts.

* Unsigned arithmetic vs signed overflow traps

Compilers emit =addu= and =addiu= for ordinary integer math because MIPS =add= and =addi= raise a hardware arithmetic-overflow trap when a signed 32-bit sum crosses $2^(31) - 1 = 2147483647$.

#+NAME: sum
#+BEGIN_SRC mips
# sum-1-to-10.asm — accumulate 1..10 in $t0
        .text
main:   li      $t0, 0                  # sum = 0
        li      $t1, 1                  # i = 1
loop:   addu    $t0, $t0, $t1           # sum += i (wraps instead of trapping)
        addiu   $t1, $t1, 1             # i++
        ble     $t1, 10, loop           # expands to slt + beq using $at
        move    $a0, $t0                # print 55
        li      $v0, 1
        syscall
        li      $a0, 10                 # '\n'
        li      $v0, 11
        syscall
        li      $v0, 10                 # exit
        syscall
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
: 55

*Try editing:* set a breakpoint on =ble $t1, 10, loop= and step through its expanded instructions to see =$at= change in *Registers*. To see a real MIPS overflow trap, replace =li $t0, 0= with =li $t0, 2147483647= and change =addu= to =add=.

* 64-bit products in =hi:lo= and =jal=/=jr= calls

Multiplying two 32-bit integers can produce up to 64 bits. Rather than writing two general registers at once, =mult= stores the full 64-bit product across dedicated =hi= and =lo= registers; you pull the halves back with =mflo= and =mfhi=.

Step into =jal fact= below and watch =$ra= capture the return address (=pc + 4=) while *hi · lo · pc* updates on every =mult=.

#+NAME: factorial
#+BEGIN_SRC mips
# fact-10.asm — factorial(10) = 3628800 via mult / mflo
        .text
main:   li      $a0, 10                 # n = 10
        jal     fact                    # $ra <- return address; pc <- fact
        move    $a0, $v0
        li      $v0, 1
        syscall
        li      $a0, 10                 # '\n'
        li      $v0, 11
        syscall
        li      $v0, 10
        syscall

fact:   li      $v0, 1                  # result = 1
        li      $t0, 2                  # i = 2
loop:   mult    $v0, $t0                # hi:lo = result * i (64 bits)
        mflo    $v0                     # result <- low 32 bits
        addiu   $t0, $t0, 1             # i++
        ble     $t0, $a0, loop          # while (i <= n)
        jr      $ra                     # return: pc <- $ra
#+END_SRC

#+RESULTS:
: 3628800

*Try editing:* change =li $a0, 10= to =li $a0, 13= and re-run. Because $13! = 6227020800 > 2^(32) - 1$, *hi · lo · pc* shows ~hi = 1~ — the overflowed upper 32 bits that =mflo= silently discarded! Compare this with [[file:c.org][32-bit overflow in C]].
